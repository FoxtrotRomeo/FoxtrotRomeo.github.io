Machine learning promises a great deal in healthcare — better diagnostic accuracy, earlier intervention, more finely tuned treatment. The obstacle to clinical adoption is not usually accuracy but opacity: a model whose reasoning cannot be inspected is a model a clinician cannot responsibly act on.

Explainable AI is the standard answer, but the field tends to evaluate its own methods against computational proxies rather than against the practitioners they are built for. This study takes the other route and interviews clinical experts about the explanations themselves — what they find legible, what they find misleading, and what they would actually need before trusting a model's output in practice.

This paper grew out of Katja Wilde's Master's thesis, which I supervised at DSV.
