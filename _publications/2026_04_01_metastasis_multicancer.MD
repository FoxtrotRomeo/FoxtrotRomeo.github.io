Metastatic spread is easier to act on the earlier it is caught, but the signals that precede it are scattered across a patient's record — laboratory values in one place, a radiologist's phrasing in another. This paper asks whether those signals can be combined into a usable early warning.

We built a framework that predicts metastasis risk one month before diagnosis, drawing on six months of preceding clinical history from electronic health records across four cancer cohorts collected at Karolinska University Hospital. The model integrates structured measurements with unstructured clinical notes rather than treating either in isolation, and we use SHAP to look at what each of the four modalities contributes to the prediction.

Work with Korbinian Randl, who led the NLP and explainability components, and Braslav Jovanovic, who provided the clinical grounding, together with my supervisors.
