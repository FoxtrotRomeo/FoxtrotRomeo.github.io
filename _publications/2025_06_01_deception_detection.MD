Most work on automated deception detection looks only at the person doing the deceiving. But a dyadic exchange has two participants, and the listener reacts — sometimes in ways that carry information the speaker's own behaviour does not.

We test that directly, using audio and video features (facial action units and gaze) from both members of the pair, and comparing early and late fusion across every combination of participant and modality. The question is not only whether deception is detectable, but where in the exchange the detectable part actually lives.

Thomas Jack Samuels and I contributed equally to the design, experiments, and writing. [Code and preprocessing pipelines are public.](https://github.com/FoxtrotRomeo/DeceptionDetection)
