Chronic kidney disease is a serious public health burden in Jalisco, Mexico, and one that is hard to study properly because the clinical data you would want does not exist in accessible form.

The workflow in this paper works around that constraint by combining public sources instead — mortality records and geospatial data — to identify demographic and geographic factors associated with CKD, with explainable AI used to interpret what the model has latched onto. It is an argument for what can still be learned when the ideal dataset is out of reach.

This paper grew out of Kevin Igor Arjona Cardenas's Master's thesis, which I supervised at DSV.
