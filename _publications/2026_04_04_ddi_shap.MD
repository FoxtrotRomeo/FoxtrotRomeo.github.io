Drug-drug interactions can produce serious adverse effects and undercut treatment that would otherwise work. Machine learning models for predicting them have improved quickly, but a prediction that cannot be interrogated is of limited use to a prescriber weighing a specific decision for a specific patient.

This case study applies SHAP to the DDI prediction problem and examines what the resulting attributions do and do not tell you — where the explanations illuminate the model's reasoning and where they fall short of what clinical interpretation would require.

This publication stems from Muna Shati's Master's thesis work.
