Sepsis kills millions of people a year and is treatable when caught in time, which makes identifying at-risk patients both urgent and hard — the condition is heterogeneous enough that the traditional scoring systems have lost much of their discriminative power.

MASICU is an architecture for predicting ICU sepsis mortality that takes interpretability as a design constraint rather than a post-hoc addition. It combines several modalities and fusion strategies, uses attention mechanisms to expose which inputs are driving a given prediction, and draws on both static and temporal features so that the model sees the patient's trajectory rather than a snapshot.

Led by Lena Mondrejevski, my work in this paper is reflected in the multimodal data integration.
