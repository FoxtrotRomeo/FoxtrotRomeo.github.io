## Modelling interaction in psychotherapy

Psychotherapy is a conversation, and what makes it work is not contained in the transcript alone. Tone, pause, gaze, and facial movement carry a great deal of what passes between therapist and patient. I use multimodal machine learning to model these dyadic interactions and to study the working alliance — the collaborative bond that turns out to be one of the more reliable predictors of treatment outcome.

This strand of my work is part of the project *Let's talk about nonverbal communication*, run jointly with the Department of Psychology at Stockholm University and funded by the Marcus and Amalia Wallenberg Foundation.

## Disease progression from electronic health records

Electronic health records contain structured measurements and unstructured clinical text side by side, usually analysed separately. I build models that use both to predict how a patient's disease will progress — in particular, the onset of metastasis in cancer patients, where catching the signal early enough to act on is the whole point.

## Explainability

A prediction a clinician cannot interrogate is a prediction they cannot responsibly use. I work on explainability methods for multimodal models, including the harder question of how to attribute a prediction across modalities rather than within a single one, and on evaluating whether the explanations we produce are actually useful to the people receiving them.
